Pump Priority Simulator

Interactive results from our solar microgrid simulation for Ancotanga, Bolivia. Each number comes from running the grid hour by hour over 20 years of NASA weather. Move the sliders to compare a community-ranked grid with a standard shared grid!!!

Altitude 3,730 m. 175,320 hours of weather. 60 homes, a water pump, a clinic, and a school. 802 hours a year below 0 °C. All results are predictions from our simulation, not measurements from Ancotanga.

What happens as families use more power?

After receiving a more constant stream of electricity, consumers will buy more electric appliances (e.g., TVs, lights, etc.). Both grids use the same hardware: 10 kW of panels and a 40 kWh battery.

Method. NASA POWER hourly weather (2005 to 2024) at latitude -17.77, longitude -67.44. Panel physics from pvlib (Erbs, Hay-Davies, and Faiman models). An LFP battery that cannot charge below 0 °C, kept in an insulated adobe room. Uptime is the share of hours a service needed power and got it.

Ranked grid: services are served in the community's order (pump, clinic, school, homes), and each can drain the battery only down to its cut-off. Shared grid: one battery for everyone, and a shortage cuts power to all services together.
